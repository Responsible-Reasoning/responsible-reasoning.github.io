The **First Workshop on Responsible Reasoning in the Wild** invites submissions that move beyond purely associative learning toward AI systems that can reason about interventions, counterfactuals, causal structure, and abstract relations — and whose behavior we can inspect, constrain, and trust.

### Submission Tracks

We invite two types of submissions:

#### (1) Research papers

Novel methods for inference and learning in neuro-symbolic systems; integration of symbolic reasoning, causal structure, or structured priors with existing ML and AI approaches; and theoretical or empirical advances that move toward AI systems with principled generalization, verifiable behavior, or interpretable representations. We particularly welcome work that addresses failure modes of purely associative systems, such as brittleness under distribution shift, susceptibility to spurious correlations, or opaque decision-making.

#### (2) Industry track papers

Contributions demonstrating responsible reasoning in real-world settings. We seek work that goes beyond proof-of-concept: papers that show where causal or neuro-symbolic methods add genuine value in production, what engineering and scaling challenges remain, and what the path toward broader adoption looks like. Negative results and honest accounts of limitations are explicitly encouraged.

### Topics

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

- **Neuro-Symbolic and Causal Methods.**
  - Deep probabilistic logic languages
  - Bayesian and relational methods
  - Rule learning and object-centric representations
  - Causal discovery and inference
  - Tractable and scalable inference (complexity, knowledge compilation, probabilistic circuits)
- **Trust & Safety in AI & ML.**
  - Symbolic constraints and guarantees for large models
  - Neuro-symbolic safeguards and verification for generative AI
  - Neuro-symbolic agents: auditable modeling, planning, and interfaces
  - Human-in-the-loop oversight through symbolic interfaces
- **Responsible Reasoning in the Wild.**
  - Evolving systems and distribution shift
  - Time series reasoning
  - Continual learning
  - Information fusion across heterogeneous sources
  - Asynchronous and interactive systems
  - Robust representations
  - Datasets and benchmarks for neuro-symbolic methods in practice

### Review and Presentation

All submissions will be reviewed for fit and quality. We are committed to an inclusive submission and review process, and will provide dedicated time during poster sessions for mentorship and informal exchange, especially for early-career researchers.
